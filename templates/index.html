How to read the colors: For Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s, colors compare the entered score change with published clinical-change references. For Huntington’s, the band classifies the magnitude and direction of the observed annual score change using app-defined cutoffs: under 1%, 1–5%, or at least 5% of the 0–124 point scale per year. These bands are not clinically validated and do not indicate disease risk or neuron loss. Results vary with rater, exam conditions, and time between reports; a clinician should interpret them.

Definitions of scores, tests, and graph terms

Formula and evidence: Standard percentage-change methods compare a follow-up score with the starting score and therefore depend on the starting value. This app instead divides by the full score range so the calculation also works when the starting score is zero. That range-normalized formula is an app-defined description, not a published biological neurodegeneration formula or validated personal estimate. The separate CDR-SB and original UPDRS Part III change references are clinical score references, not thresholds for neuron loss.

Clinical score percentage formula: (most recent score − earlier score) ÷ full score range × 100 ÷ years between reports. The result is signed % of the scale range per year. It is a transparent, app-defined normalization of the test score—not a published biological formula, percent neurodegeneration, percent neuron loss, prognosis, or personal risk. The separate feature summary averages absolute changes in entered non-clinical results, each divided by its app entry range and elapsed years. A treatment line, when available, shows a group average for context only—not an expected personal response.
