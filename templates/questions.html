    About neurodegenerative diseases

    Does a symptom or a score by itself confirm a disease?

    No. A clinical score describes specific findings captured by a particular assessment. Diagnosis depends on a qualified clinician’s evaluation, which may include history, examination, appropriate tests, and consideration of other causes.

    Does a higher score always mean the same thing across diseases?

    No. Each scale has its own items, range, direction, and meaning. This dashboard uses CDR-SB for Alzheimer’s, UPDRS Part III for Parkinson’s, and UHDRS Total Motor Score for Huntington’s. Values across different scales are not directly comparable.

    What does the Alzheimer’s CDR-SB score show?

    The Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes is a clinician-rated measure across memory, orientation, judgment/problem solving, community affairs, home/hobbies, and personal care. The dashboard’s 0–18 range uses higher scores for greater impairment; interpretation depends on clinical context and the assessment.

    What does the Parkinson’s UPDRS Part III score show?

    It rates motor signs observed during an examination. The original UPDRS Part III ranges from 0 to 108, with higher scores reflecting more motor signs recorded. Medication state, exam conditions, and the exact scale version matter; MDS-UPDRS and UPDRS scores should not be mixed.

    What does the Huntington’s UHDRS Total Motor Score show?

    It records motor findings assessed by a trained rater; this dashboard uses a 0–124 range, with higher values indicating more motor signs recorded. It does not by itself summarize all Huntington’s symptoms, establish stage, or predict an individual’s course.

    Are the dashboard’s annualized rates progression forecasts?

    No. They summarize the difference between the two values entered divided by the interval entered. A straight-line extension is a mathematical illustration, not a validated forecast of an individual’s future disease course or neuron loss.

    Why can scores change between assessments?

    Observed scores can vary with symptoms, rater, testing conditions, medication timing, fatigue, and the version or administration of the test. A change should be reviewed in clinical context, not read as a standalone diagnosis or treatment signal.

    About biomarkers and protein results

    Can I compare protein concentrations from two reports?

    Only if the reports measure the same analyte using comparable assay methods, specimen type, units, and collection/processing conditions. Different platforms and laboratories can use different calibration and reference intervals. This dashboard does not harmonize assays or apply clinically validated thresholds.

    Does a biomarker change mean the disease has progressed?

    Not necessarily. The biological meaning depends on the specific marker, assay, specimen, clinical indication, and validated interpretation for that test. An unadjusted numeric difference in this app is descriptive only and does not indicate disease progression or treatment response.

    Why do you ask for the assay name and units?

    A number without its test method, specimen, units, and laboratory reference interval can be misleading. Use the complete original lab report and ask the ordering clinician to interpret the result.

    Are the Parkinson’s voice features routine biomarkers?

    No. The dataset includes voice-analysis measures collected for research. They are not interchangeable with a clinical motor examination, and this prototype’s normalized feature index is not a validated diagnostic, progression, or treatment-response measure.

    About genetic results

    Does selecting a genotype in the dashboard mean I have it?

    No. The variant explorer contains illustrative examples only; it is not connected to genetic testing. Only a qualified laboratory report can establish a person’s genotype or repeat count.

    Does APOE ε4 cause Alzheimer’s disease?

    APOE ε4 is associated with increased late-onset Alzheimer disease risk, but it is neither necessary nor sufficient for the disease. A result cannot tell whether a person will develop Alzheimer’s or predict exact onset and course.

    What does an HTT CAG repeat range mean?

    As general categories, 26 or fewer repeats are in the normal range; 27–35 is an intermediate range that does not cause Huntington disease in the carrier but may be unstable in transmission; 36–39 is reduced penetrance; and 40 or more is generally considered full penetrance. Exact interpretation requires a clinical laboratory report and genetic counseling. Repeat count does not predict an individual’s exact onset.

    Do LRRK2 or GBA1 variants guarantee Parkinson’s disease?

    No. LRRK2-associated disease has incomplete penetrance. GBA1 variants can increase Parkinson disease probability but do not make it certain; biallelic disease-causing GBA1 variants can cause Gaucher disease. The exact variant and whether one or both gene copies are affected matter.

    About care and treatment

    Can these results tell me which treatment to use?

    No. This research prototype does not evaluate eligibility, treatment benefit, contraindications, or individualized care. Discuss options, risks, benefits, and monitoring with your clinician. Do not start, stop, or change treatment based on this site.

    When should I seek medical help?

    For new, worsening, or concerning symptoms, contact a healthcare professional who knows the person’s history. For an immediate emergency, use local emergency services.
